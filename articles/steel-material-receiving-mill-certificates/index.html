Steel material receiving inspection and mill test certificates (EN 10204 3.1)

By Hicham Zein Eddine · Steel inspection reference note · the guide itself covers the foundation stage

Short answer: Match every delivery to its mill test certificate: grade, heat number, chemical composition and mechanical results must match the specification, and the heat number on the piece must match the certificate. Then check marking, dimensions, straightness and damage, and quarantine anything that does not match.

Receiving checklist

  1. Delivery note matches the order and the drawing marks.
  2. Mill test certificate (EN 10204 type 3.1 where specified) for each heat.
  3. Heat number on the steel matches the certificate.
  4. Grade meets the specification (for example ASTM A992, A572 Gr 50 or EN 10025 S355).
  5. Section size and plate thickness checked with a tape and calliper.
  6. No bends, dents, lamination or coating damage from transport.
  7. Bolts, nuts and washers: grade markings, lot numbers, certificates.
  8. Anchor rods: grade colour code (ASTM F1554 Grade 36 blue, 55 yellow, 105 red), thread length, galvanizing.

What to do with a mismatch

Tag it, keep it apart, and write a non-conformance report. Do not install material without a matching certificate.

The same check applies to reinforcement before it goes into the foundation: hold point 5.

Questions engineers ask

What is an EN 10204 3.1 certificate?
An inspection certificate issued by the manufacturer's authorized inspector, independent of production, with actual test results for the delivered product.
What is the colour code for ASTM F1554 anchor rods?
Grade 36 blue, Grade 55 yellow, Grade 105 red, on the end of the rod.
What if the heat number does not match the certificate?
Quarantine the material and raise a non-conformance report; do not install it.
