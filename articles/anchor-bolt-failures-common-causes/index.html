Why anchor bolts fail on site: 9 common causes and how to prevent them

By Hicham Zein Eddine · From the author's live workshop

Short answer: Most anchor-bolt failures on site come from nine causes: wrong drawing revision, no template, setting out from formwork, bolts not fixed rigidly, concrete pumped at the bolts, wrong grade or diameter, unprotected threads, damage before erection and unapproved fixes such as bending or heating. Each one is stopped by a hold point before the pour.
CausePrevention
1. Wrong drawing revisionCheck the bolt plan against the latest issued-for-construction drawings and the steel shop drawings.
2. No templateOne template per bolt pattern, drilled to the base plate detail.
3. Setting out from the formworkSet out from survey control with offset pegs.
4. Bolts tied but not fixedTie, check level and alignment, then fix rigidly as specified.
5. Concrete aimed at the boltsDirect the pump onto the reinforcement; vibrate away from the assembly.
6. Wrong grade or diameterMeasure every bolt with a caliper and check the grade marking.
7. Concrete on the threadsTape or cap the threads before the pour.
8. Damage before erectionCaps, marking and keeping equipment away.
9. Unapproved fixesNo bending, heating or cutting without the structural engineer.

None of these needs advanced calculation. They need someone with the authority to stop the pour until every check is signed.

Questions engineers ask

What is the most common cause of misplaced anchor bolts?
Setting out from the wrong reference or without a rigid template, so the bolt group moves before or during the pour.
How do you prevent anchor bolt problems?
With hold points before the pour: drawing check, survey, template, fixing, thread protection and sign-off.
Who decides the repair when anchor bolts fail?
The structural engineer.
