High-strength bolt tightening inspection: snug-tight, turn-of-nut, DTI and TC bolts

By Hicham Zein Eddine · Steel inspection reference note · the guide itself covers the foundation stage

Short answer: First confirm the joint type on the drawings: snug-tight, pretensioned or slip-critical. Pretensioned joints are tightened by one of four methods (turn-of-nut, calibrated wrench, twist-off tension-control bolts or direct tension indicators), and the inspector verifies the method with a pre-installation check on site, not by torquing finished bolts at random.

Three joint types

  • Snug-tight: plies in firm contact, tightened by a few impacts of an impact wrench or the full effort of an ironworker with an ordinary spud wrench.
  • Pretensioned: bolts brought to a minimum tension by an approved method.
  • Slip-critical: pretensioned, plus faying surfaces prepared to the specified slip class (no paint or galvanizing unless qualified).

Four pretensioning methods (RCSC Specification)

MethodWhat the inspector watches
Turn-of-nutSnug first, match-mark nut and bolt, then the specified rotation (1/3 turn for bolt length up to 4 diameters, 1/2 turn over 4 to 8, 2/3 turn over 8 to 12, for faces normal to the axis)
Calibrated wrenchDaily calibration with a tension calibrator (Skidmore-Wilhelm) for each lot, diameter and length
Twist-off (TC) boltsSpline sheared off; bolts pre-checked in the tension calibrator
Direct tension indicator (DTI)Feeler-gauge refusals in the specified number of gaps

Material checks before any bolting

Bolt grade marking (ASTM F3125 Grade A325 or A490, or ISO property class 8.8 / 10.9 under EN 14399), nut and washer grades, galvanized or plain as specified, and storage: bolts kept dry and lubricated, not left loose in the rain.

Common mistakes

  • Checking torque on finished bolts as the acceptance test.
  • Reusing pretensioned A490 or galvanized bolts.
  • Tightening from the edges of a large joint inwards instead of from the stiffest part outwards.

Anchor rods in the foundation are a different item: they are usually snug-tight unless the drawings say otherwise. See ASTM F1554 grades.

Questions engineers ask

What is the turn-of-nut method?
The bolt is first snug-tightened, nut and bolt are match-marked, then the nut is rotated a specified amount depending on bolt length, for example 1/3 turn for lengths up to 4 diameters.
Can you check pretension with a torque wrench after installation?
Not as the acceptance test. The RCSC approach is a pre-installation verification of the method, then routine observation that the method is followed.
Can A490 bolts be reused?
No. A490 bolts and galvanized A325 bolts must not be reused once pretensioned.
