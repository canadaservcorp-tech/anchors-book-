SRC vs OPC: which cement for foundations?

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 11 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: Concrete below zero level is commonly ordered with Sulfate-Resisting Cement (SRC), because it is in contact with sulfates in the soil; concrete above ground uses Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC). Always confirm against the drawings, specification and geotechnical report.

Two things must be right when you order concrete for foundations and beams: the grade and the cement type. The wrong cement is invisible on pour day: a foundation in OPC below ground looks identical to one in SRC. Underground, over the years, it is exposed to exactly the attack it was not made to resist.

Testing the concrete

The guide follows cube testing (BS EN 12390), standard in the Gulf, Europe and much of the Middle East. ACI/ASTM jurisdictions use cylinders (ASTM C31/C39). Use whichever your project specifies, and never mix the two acceptance values: cube and cylinder strengths are not equal.

Low 28-day result?

Notify the designer immediately and never hide a failed result. The designer may re-check capacity, call for core testing, or require strengthening.

Questions engineers ask

Why use sulfate-resisting cement below ground?
Because buried concrete is in contact with sulfates and salts in the soil that attack ordinary cement over time.
What concrete grade is typical for footings?
Commonly 30 N to 35 N, confirmed on the drawings and specification.
Are cube and cylinder strengths the same?
No. Use the test your project specifies and do not mix the acceptance values.
