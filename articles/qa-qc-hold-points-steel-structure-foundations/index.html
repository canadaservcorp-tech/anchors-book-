QA/QC hold points for steel-structure foundations and anchor bolts (10-stage plan)

By Hicham Zein Eddine · From Chapter 14 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: A steel-structure foundation has 10 hold points: survey control, excavation and compaction, polyethylene and anti-termite, blinding level, rebar against mill certificates, anchor-bolt setting, the pre-concrete final gate, fresh-concrete tests, the 7-day cubes and the 28-day cubes. Each stage is signed by a named person and leaves a record; no stage is passed without it.

Quality is not inspected into the work; it must be built into the work. The QA/QC plan turns that idea into hold points: stages the site cannot pass until someone has inspected them, signed, and filed a record. The guide references this plan to ACI 318 Chapter 26 and ISO 9001.

Three rules that make the plan work

  • Verify at each stage. A defect found at blinding costs a few hours; the same defect found at erection costs a cut bolt and a post-installed anchor.
  • Document what you verify. A check without a record did not happen, as far as the client and the consultant are concerned.
  • Keep it traceable. Mill certificates, delivery tickets and cube reports must link to the element they belong to.

The final gate: hold point 7

Before concrete, everything is checked one last time: latest IFC drawings, bolt alignment and position, formwork, rebar and cover, anchor grade and diameter, template locked and welded to the rebar, debris blown out and washed, threads taped, safe access, lighting and a tested vibrator. Then the consultant or client signs. The free printable checklist has every line.

When a result fails

If the 28-day result comes back below the specified grade, notify the designer immediately and never hide it. The designer decides: a capacity re-check, core testing, or strengthening.

Questions engineers ask

What are the hold points for a steel-structure foundation?
Survey control, excavation and compaction, polyethylene and anti-termite treatment, blinding level, rebar against mill certificates, anchor-bolt setting, the pre-concrete final gate, fresh-concrete tests, and the 7-day and 28-day cube results.
Who signs the pre-concrete final gate?
The consultant or the client, after a formal inspection. No signature, no pour.
Which standards does a foundation QA/QC plan reference?
The guide references ACI 318 Chapter 26 and ISO 9001; your project specification and ITP govern.
What does a QA/QC engineer record for rebar?
Grade and diameter checked against the mill certificates, before reinforcement is fixed.
