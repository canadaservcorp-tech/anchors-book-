Base plate grouting: why it must be non-shrink grout

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 12 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: The base plate sits on levelling nuts above the concrete, and the gap is filled with a non-shrink grout. The grout is structural: it transfers the column load into the foundation, and ordinary mortar cracks under wind and column movement.

Setting the base plate

  • The base plate is not seated directly on the concrete. It sits on levelling nuts (or shims), leaving a deliberate gap.
  • That gap lets you level the plate and plumb the column before anything is permanent.
  • Once levelled, plumbed and tightened, the gap is ready for grout.

Why non-shrink

A steel structure is never perfectly still. Wind and column movement put constant small forces into the base. Ordinary or shrinking mortar cracks, loses contact with the plate, and the connection loosens. Non-shrink grout fills the gap without shrinking and stays intact under vibration.

  • Use the specified non-shrink grout, never site-mixed ordinary mortar.
  • Fill the gap completely, with no voids.
  • Follow the specified mixing and placing method.

Grout voids under the plate

Symptom: a hollow sound when the plate is tapped. Confirm a non-shrink grout to ASTM C1107 was used. For large gaps over 50 mm, use a coarse-aggregate or epoxy grout. Fill deep voids by pressure injection. Small voids may be acceptable; large voids require removal and re-grouting.

Questions engineers ask

Why use non-shrink grout under a base plate?
Because it fills the gap without shrinking and does not crack under wind and column movement, so the load keeps transferring into the foundation.
What standard covers non-shrink grout?
ASTM C1107, packaged dry hydraulic-cement grout.
How do you detect voids under a base plate?
Tap the plate: a hollow sound means the grout did not fill the gap.
