Anchor bolt setting tolerances: what is acceptable?

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 12 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: Typical values are ±6 mm for the bolt group centre from the column centre-line, ±3 mm between bolts within a group, +25 mm / −6 mm for projection (per detail) and ±13 mm for bolt top elevation. Always use the tolerances in your own drawings and specification.
ItemTypical tolerance
Bolt group centre from column centre-line±6 mm
Spacing between individual bolts within a group±3 mm
Bolt projection above concrete+25 mm / −6 mm (per detail)
Bolt top elevation±13 mm
Base plate levelCorrected on levelling nuts before grouting

Table 12.1 of the guide. Values are representative of AISC (Code of Standard Practice / Design Guide 1) and ACI 117 anchorage tolerances. Tolerances differ between codes and projects.

Check them before the pour, not after

Between fixing the bolts and pouring, a lot happens around them: rebar is adjusted, people and tools move through. At the final gate, check again:

  • Top level of each bolt: consistent across the group so the base plate seats flat. Adjust with the nuts.
  • Spacing: the distances still match the pattern.
  • Alignment: the group is squared to the lines and centred on the surveyed column point, using the centre nail.

If bolts are out after casting

Under 6 mm, the oversized base plate holes can often take it. From 6 to 12 mm, consult the structural engineer. Over 12 mm, do not bend the bolts. See misaligned anchor bolts.

Questions engineers ask

What is the typical tolerance for anchor bolt spacing?
±3 mm between bolts within a group, and ±6 mm for the group centre from the column centre-line. Your project specification governs.
How much can anchor bolts project above the concrete?
A typical tolerance is +25 mm / −6 mm on the projection shown on the detail.
How is the base plate levelled?
On levelling nuts (or shims) on the bolts, leaving a gap under the plate that is then filled with non-shrink grout.
