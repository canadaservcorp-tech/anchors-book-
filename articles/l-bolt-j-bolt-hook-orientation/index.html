L-bolt and J-bolt hooks: which way should they face?

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 11 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: The hook of an L-bolt or J-bolt always faces the external side of the foundation, away from the centre. Each bolt gets two nuts and two washers, one set below the template and one above, and is embedded to the depth on the drawing.

Hooks face outward

The bent leg of the hook grips the concrete and resists pull-out. Turned outward, away from the centre, it anchors the way the design assumed. Set the wrong way, the bolt looks the same from above, but it is not anchored as designed. This matters most where uplift and overturning govern: PEB portals under wind suction, cantilevered poles and open canopies.

Two nuts and two washers on every bolt

One nut and washer below the template, one above. The bolt is gripped from both sides, so it cannot move during the pour, and you can raise or lower it with the nuts to set the projection exactly.

Embedment comes from the drawing

Embedment depth is a structural value: it lets the bolt develop its capacity. Read it from the drawing and set it exactly. A common field rule of thumb is about 10 × the diameter minimum, with edge distance about 5 × the diameter, but these are not code minimums. ACI 318 Chapter 17 or EN 1992-4 calculations govern.

Tie, check, then weld

  1. Tie the anchors to the rebar with binding wire: held, but still adjustable.
  2. Check level and alignment, and adjust until it is exactly right.
  3. Weld the anchors to the reinforcement with horizontal rebar, so pump pressure cannot move them.

Weld before you check, and you lock in an error. Rely on binding wire alone, and the pour will move your bolts.

Questions engineers ask

Which way does a J-bolt hook face?
Toward the outside of the foundation, away from the centre, so it resists pull-out as designed.
How many nuts go on an anchor bolt during casting?
Two nuts and two washers: one set below the template and one above.
Is binding wire enough to hold anchor bolts during the pour?
No. Tie first to adjust, check, then weld the anchors to the rebar with horizontal bars.
