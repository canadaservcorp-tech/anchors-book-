Post-installed anchors: chemical vs mechanical vs undercut

By Hicham Zein Eddine · From Chapter 13 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: Post-installed anchors are drilled and fixed into concrete that has already cured (ACI 318 Chapter 17, EN 1992-4). Chemical anchors bond a bolt with injected epoxy or vinylester resin (ACI 355.4, ETAG 001); mechanical expansion anchors expand against the hole (ACI 355.2); undercut anchors expand into a shaped undercut for the highest capacity (ACI 355.2). They are a repair or an addition, never a substitute for correctly set cast-in bolts.

Cast-in anchor bolts, set before the concrete is poured, are always the strongest and preferred method. Post-installed anchors are for situations where that was not possible: bolts were missed, a new attachment must be added to hardened concrete, or a repair is needed.

The three types

TypeHow it worksKey standards
Chemical (adhesive)Epoxy or vinylester resin is injected into a drilled hole; the bolt is inserted and the resin cures to bond it.ACI 355.4, ETAG 001
Mechanical expansionA wedge- or sleeve-type anchor expands against the hole.ACI 355.2
UndercutThe anchor expands into an undercut at the base of a specially shaped hole, for the highest capacity.ACI 355.2

Installation rules

  • Edge distance and spacing: meet the manufacturer's and ACI 318 minimums. Too close to an edge and the concrete can blow out under load.
  • Clean holes for chemical anchors: correct hole diameter, brush and compressed air twice, the specified volume of adhesive with no air gaps. A dirty hole is the most common cause of adhesive-anchor failure.
  • Exact torque for mechanical anchors: under-torqued, the anchor slips; over-torqued, it damages the concrete or the anchor.
  • Load testing: for critical applications, field pull-out tests (ASTM E488) may be required before the connection is accepted.
A post-installed anchor is a repair or an addition, not a substitute for planning. A correctly set cast-in bolt is always stronger and cheaper than fixing a missed one later.

Questions engineers ask

Are chemical anchors as strong as cast-in anchor bolts?
Cast-in bolts set before the pour are always the strongest and preferred method. Post-installed anchors are for repairs and additions, designed per ACI 318 Chapter 17 or EN 1992-4.
What is the most common cause of chemical anchor failure?
A dirty hole. Clean it thoroughly with a brush and compressed air, twice, before injecting the adhesive.
How are post-installed anchors tested on site?
For critical applications, by field pull-out tests per ASTM E488 when the specification requires them.
