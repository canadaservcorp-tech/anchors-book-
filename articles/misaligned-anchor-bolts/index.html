Misaligned anchor bolts after casting: the correct fixes

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 15 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: Under 6 mm: the base plate holes are normally oversized, so the plate can often shift. From 6 to 12 mm: consult the structural engineer, who may allow slotted holes with thick hardened plate washers. Over 12 mm: do not bend the bolts; cut them off and install post-installed anchors, with the engineer's approval.

Symptom: the bolt group does not line up with the base plate holes. What separates a strong engineer is not never having a problem; it is knowing the correct fix, and knowing which problems must go back to the designer.

Minor: less than 6 mm

Check the base plate first. Plate holes are normally oversized, typically bolt diameter + 3 to 6 mm, so the plate can often shift to take the bolts.

Moderate: 6 to 12 mm

Consult the structural engineer. They may permit slotting (elongating) the plate holes, with thick hardened plate washers over them.

Severe: more than 12 mm

Do not bend the bolts. Bending reduces their tensile capacity. The accepted fix is to cut off the bolts and install post-installed anchors next to the originals. Structural engineer approval required.

Prevent it

Most misalignment starts before the pour: no template, bolts tied but not welded, or the pump aimed at the bolts. Tie, check level and alignment, then weld to the rebar, and direct the concrete onto the reinforcement, never at the bolt assembly.

Questions engineers ask

Can you bend anchor bolts to fit the base plate?
No. Bending reduces their tensile capacity. Beyond 12 mm, cut them off and install post-installed anchors with the structural engineer's approval.
How oversized are base plate holes?
Typically the bolt diameter plus 3 to 6 mm, which is why small misalignments can often be absorbed.
Who approves a fix for misaligned anchor bolts?
The structural engineer, for anything from 6 mm upward.
