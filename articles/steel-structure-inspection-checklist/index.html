Steel structure inspection checklist for QA/QC inspectors (receiving to final bolting)

By Hicham Zein Eddine · Steel inspection reference note · the guide itself covers the foundation stage

Short answer: A steel-structure inspection runs in six stages: as-built survey of the anchor bolts before erection, material receiving against mill certificates, erection and plumbness, bolting, welding, then coatings and touch-up. Each stage is checked against the project specification and the referenced standard, and each leaves a signed record.

Steel is inspected at every hand-over: from the foundation crew to the erector, from the fabricator to the site, and from the erector to the painter. A defect found at the right stage costs a phone call; found after the cladding is on, it costs a crane.

The 6 stages

StageWhat the inspector checksTypical referenceRecord
1. Before erectionAs-built survey of anchor-bolt positions, projection and top-of-grout levelsProject tolerances, AISC Code of Standard PracticeAnchor-bolt as-built survey

5. WeldingWelder qualification, WPS, visual inspection, NDT as specifiedAWS D1.1 / ISO 5817 / CSA W59Weld inspection and NDT reports
6. CoatingsSurface preparation, dry film thickness, galvanizing damage repairSSPC / ISO 8501-1 / ISO 19840 / ISO 1461Coating inspection report

Start with the foundation

Most erection problems begin at the anchor bolts. Before the first column arrives, survey every bolt group: position, projection above concrete and thread condition. A bolt that is 20 mm out is a fix on paper today and a problem with a column hanging on the crane tomorrow. The guide's pre-pour checklist and tolerance table cover that stage.

What governs

The project specification and the inspection and test plan (ITP) govern. The standards in the table are the common references; the project names the ones that apply and the acceptance criteria.

Questions engineers ask

What are the stages of steel structure inspection?
Anchor-bolt survey before erection, material receiving, erection and plumbness, bolting, welding, and coatings.
Which standard governs steel erection inspection?
The project specification. Common references are the AISC Code of Standard Practice (AISC 303) in North America and EN 1090-2 in Europe.
Why survey anchor bolts before steel erection?
Because misplaced bolts are cheapest to correct before a column is lifted, and the correction must be approved by the structural engineer.
