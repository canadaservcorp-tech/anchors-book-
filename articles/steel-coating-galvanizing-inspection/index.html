Steel coating and galvanizing inspection: surface preparation, DFT and repair

By Hicham Zein Eddine · Steel inspection reference note · the guide itself covers the foundation stage

Short answer: Coating inspection checks four things: surface preparation grade and profile before painting, ambient conditions (dew point), dry film thickness with a calibrated gauge to SSPC-PA 2 or ISO 19840, and repair of damage after erection. Galvanized steel is checked for coating thickness to ASTM A123 or ISO 1461, and damaged areas are repaired as the project specifies.

Before painting

  • Surface preparation grade: for example Sa 2½ (ISO 8501-1) or SSPC-SP 10 near-white blast.
  • Surface profile measured with replica tape or a profile gauge.
  • Steel temperature at least 3 °C above the dew point (common rule in coating specifications).

Dry film thickness (DFT)

Measured with a calibrated magnetic gauge. SSPC-PA 2 sets the number and location of readings in North America. ISO 19840 is often used with an acceptance rule that no more than 20% of readings fall below nominal and none below 80% of nominal, unless the specification says otherwise.

Galvanizing

  • Coating thickness against ASTM A123 or ISO 1461 tables for the steel thickness.
  • No bare spots, dross or flux inclusions.
  • Damaged areas repaired with zinc-rich paint or another method the specification accepts (ASTM A780 in the USA).

Galvanized anchor rods follow ASTM F2329 / A153; check that nuts were tapped oversize after galvanizing so they run freely.

Questions engineers ask

What is Sa 2½?
A near-white blast-cleaning grade defined in ISO 8501-1, equivalent in practice to SSPC-SP 10.
How is dry film thickness measured?
With a calibrated magnetic gauge, following SSPC-PA 2 or ISO 19840 for the number of readings and acceptance.
How is damaged galvanizing repaired?
With zinc-rich paint, zinc spray or solder as the specification allows; ASTM A780 covers repair methods in the USA.
