Anchor bolt and foundation standards worldwide: ACI, Eurocode, AISC, ASTM, ISO, BS EN

By Hicham Zein Eddine · From the author's live workshop

Short answer: Concrete design: ACI 318 or EN 1992-1-1. Anchor (fastening) design: ACI 318 Chapter 17 or EN 1992-4. Base plates: AISC Design Guide 1 or EN 1993-1-8. Bolt material: ASTM F1554 (Grades 36, 55, 105) or ISO 898-1 classes 8.8 / 10.9. Galvanising: ASTM A153 / A123 or ISO 1461. Grout: ASTM C1107. Concrete tests: BS EN 12350 / 12390 or ASTM C143 / C39. Use the edition your project specification names.

The guide lists these standards on page 4. Projects specify one family or the other; cite the edition applicable to your jurisdiction, and the local project specification wins wherever it differs.

Two rules on site

  • Do not mix systems on one bolt. A bolt is ASTM F1554 or ISO 898-1, not both. Match whichever system your drawings use.
  • Rules of thumb are not code minimums. Embedment around 10 × diameter and edge distance around 5 × diameter are field rules; ACI 318 Chapter 17 or EN 1992-4 calculations for the actual load case govern.

Other national codes

Many countries publish their own concrete and steel codes (for example CSA A23.3 and CSA S16 in Canada, IS 456 and IS 800 in India). The guide does not cover them; the site sequence and hold points are the same, and the numbers come from your project specification.

Questions engineers ask

Which standard covers anchor bolt grades?
ASTM F1554 (Grades 36, 55, 105) in the American system; ISO 898-1 property classes such as 8.8 and 10.9 in the metric system.
Which code governs anchor design in concrete?
ACI 318 Chapter 17 or EN 1992-4, depending on the project.
What is AISC Design Guide 1?
The AISC design guide for base plates and anchor rods.
Which standard applies to non-shrink grout under base plates?
ASTM C1107 (packaged dry, hydraulic-cement grout, non-shrink).
