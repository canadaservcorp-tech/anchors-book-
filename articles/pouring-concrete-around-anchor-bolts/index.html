Pouring concrete around anchor bolts without moving them

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 11 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: Direct the pump discharge onto the foundation rebar and let the concrete rise around the anchors; never aim it at the bolts. Order the full quantity and pour continuously, stopping only at a construction joint shown on the drawing.

Never aim the pump at the anchor bolts

Concrete leaves the pump line with real force. Aimed straight at the anchor assembly, it can push the bolts out of alignment, spacing or level, despite the fixing and welding.

  • Direct the concrete onto the reinforcement and let it flow around the bolts.
  • Keep the discharge away from the bolt assembly.
  • Let the concrete rise around the anchors gently.

Order enough to pour it all at once

A continuous pour is monolithic. Stop partway and let the concrete start to set, and the next pour does not bond as one: you create a cold joint, an unplanned line of weakness. The only correct place to stop is a construction joint provided by the design. Planned joint: stop. No joint: pour it all in one.

The pour cannot be paused

Fuel the vibrator before you start: one that dies mid-pour leaves concrete arriving that cannot be compacted, with a risk of voids and honeycombing. If the pour runs into the night, set up the lighting first.

Concrete grade and cement type

For foundations and beams, the grade is commonly 30 N or 35 N, confirmed on the drawings. Below ground, order Sulfate-Resisting Cement (SRC); above ground, Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC), subject to the specification and the geotechnical report. See SRC vs OPC.

Questions engineers ask

Where should the concrete pump be aimed near anchor bolts?
Onto the foundation reinforcement, never directly at the bolts, so pump pressure does not shift them.
What is a cold joint?
An unplanned joint where fresh concrete meets concrete that has started to set, creating a line of weakness.
When is it acceptable to stop a pour?
Only at a construction joint provided by the design.
