Concrete testing for foundations: slump, temperature and 7/28-day cubes

By Hicham Zein Eddine · From Chapter 14 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: On delivery, check the ticket, record slump (BS EN 12350 / ASTM C143) and temperature (BS EN 206 / ASTM C1064), and cast cubes: typically 3 per sample, broken at 7, 14 and 28 days (BS EN 12390 / ASTM C39). The 28-day result must reach the specified grade; a low result goes straight to the designer.

Appendix B of the guide. Ready-mix specification and delivery: BS EN 206 / ASTM C94. In-place soil density before the foundation: sand cone, ASTM D1556.

At the truck

  • Check the delivery ticket against the mix ordered, and keep it: it is the record for hold point 8.
  • Record slump and temperature before the concrete is accepted.
  • Cast and label the cubes so each result traces back to the element poured.

A low 28-day result

Example: 25 MPa against a specified 30 MPa. Notify the designer immediately; never hide a failed result. The designer may re-check the foundation at the lower strength, call for cores drilled from the hardened concrete for a true in-situ value, and, if it is genuinely insufficient, require strengthening such as an enlarged footing or bonded steel plates.

Questions engineers ask

How many cubes are taken per concrete sample?
Typically 3 cubes per sample, broken at 7, 14 and 28 days (BS EN 12390); follow your project specification.
What slump is acceptable for foundation concrete?
Whatever the project specification states, for example 75-100 mm, measured to BS EN 12350 or ASTM C143.
What happens if the 28-day cube result is low?
The designer is notified at once and decides: capacity re-check, core testing, or strengthening.
