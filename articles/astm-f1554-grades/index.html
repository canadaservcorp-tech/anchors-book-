ASTM F1554 Grade 36 vs 55 vs 105: which anchor bolt grade?

By Hicham Zein Eddine, Eng. · From Chapter 11 of the Anchor Bolt Field Guide

Short answer: ASTM F1554 defines three anchor bolt grades by yield strength: Grade 36 (250 MPa), Grade 55 (380 MPa) and Grade 105 (720 MPa). The grade is set by the design; on site you verify the grade and measure the diameter of every bolt against the drawing.

Anchor bolts must be checked against the design specification. The grade matters as much as the diameter, because it sets the bolt's strength.

ASTM F1554 gradeYield strengthCommon use
Grade 36250 MPa (low-strength carbon steel)Light structures, non-seismic
Grade 55380 MPa (medium strength)General industrial, PEB
Grade 105720 MPa (high strength)Heavy loads, seismic, high tension / uplift

Table 11.1 of the guide. The design decides the grade, not the site.

F1554 or ISO 8.8 / 10.9?

ASTM F1554 (Grades 36, 55, 105) and the ISO 898-1 metric property classes (such as 8.8 and 10.9) are two separate classification systems. A bolt is specified under one or the other, not both. Match whichever system your drawings use. Hooked bolts are typically hot-dip galvanised to ASTM A153 / ISO 1461.

Measure the diameter with a caliper

You cannot tell a 12 mm bolt from a 14 mm bolt by looking at it, or a 22 mm from a 24 mm. They look identical in the hand. A 2 mm difference is the difference between the right bolt and the wrong one: cast the wrong diameter and the base plate holes no longer match the foundation.

  • Measure each bolt with a caliper before it goes into the template.
  • Confirm it matches the diameter on the drawing, the same one the template holes and the base plate are made to.
  • Do it yourself. This is a check you make personally, not one you take on someone's word.

Questions engineers ask

What is the yield strength of ASTM F1554 Grade 55?
380 MPa. Grade 36 is 250 MPa and Grade 105 is 720 MPa.
Is an F1554 Grade 105 bolt the same as an ISO 10.9 bolt?
No. ASTM F1554 grades and ISO 898-1 property classes are separate systems. Use the system your drawings specify.
Why measure anchor bolt diameter on site?
Because a 2 mm difference, such as 22 mm against 24 mm, cannot be seen by eye, and the wrong diameter will not match the base plate holes.
